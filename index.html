Welcome to my personal website!

I am an undergraduate student at Qingdao University in China, majoring in International Business. My academic journey is deeply rooted in the social sciences, specifically exploring the rich intersections of sociolinguistics and linguistic anthropology.

My primary research focuses on linguistic hybridity and identity politics. Through multi-sited ethnographic fieldwork, I conduct comparative studies across multiple age cohorts—particularly to understand how dialect interaction reflects broader socioeconomic shifts.

Beyond my core research, I am actively involved in global academic dialogues, having presented my findings at international conferences in Tokyo, the UAE, and the US.

Research Projects

Political Communication & Visual Politics
Project 01

Faces vs. Steel: A Comparative Visual Framing Analysis of China's Aid in Africa

This independent research project explores the polarized visual narratives of China's aid in Africa. By conducting a mixed-methods visual framing analysis on 250+ images from Western and Chinese media, the study reveals a stark contrast: Western media is dominated by "Pure Objects," depicting a dehumanized "Technocratic Presence" (Steel), while Chinese media significantly emphasizes "Human Warmth" (Faces) to construct a softer national image.

Advised by: Prof. Maria Repnikova (Georgia State University)
